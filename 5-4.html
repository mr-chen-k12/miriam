Lesson 85 Solutions

Reference Graph for Problems 1-3: Books Read by Students
Problem 1
Which student has read exactly twice as many books as Jay?
Answer: Maria

Explanation: Looking at the graph, Jay has read 5 books. Twice as many as 5 is calculated by multiplying by 2: \( 5 \times 2 = 10 \). The graph shows that Maria is the student who has read exactly 10 books.

Problem 2
Hiroshi's goal is to read 10 books. How many more books does he need to read to reach his goal?
Answer: 4 more books

Explanation: According to the graph, Hiroshi has currently read 6 books. To find out how many more he needs to reach 10, subtract his current amount from his goal: \( 10 - 6 = 4 \).

Problem 3
If the books Annie has read have an average of 160 pages each, how many pages has she read?
Answer: 1,440 pages

Explanation: The graph shows Annie has read 9 books. If each book averages 160 pages, multiply the number of books by the pages per book: \( 9 \times 160 = 1440 \).

Problem 4
Jim saw some pentagons. The pentagons had a total of 100 sides. How many pentagons did Jim see?
Answer: 20 pentagons

Explanation: A single pentagon has 5 sides. To find the total number of pentagons, divide the total number of sides by the number of sides per pentagon: \( 100 \div 5 = 20 \).

Problem 5
Sophia bought a rectangular piece of land that was 3 miles long and 2 miles wide. Fifty percent of the land could be farmed. How many square miles could be farmed?
Answer: 3 square miles

Explanation: First, find the total area of the rectangular land: Area = length × width = \( 3 \times 2 = 6 \) square miles. Fifty percent (or half) of the land can be farmed. Half of 6 is: \( 6 \times 0.5 = 3 \) square miles.

Problem 6
Max bought 10 pencils for 24¢ each. The tax was 14¢. What was the total cost of the pencils?
Answer: $2.54 (or 254¢)

Explanation: Calculate the base cost of the pencils: \( 10 \times 24\text{¢} = 240\text{¢} \). Then, add the tax to the base cost: \( 240\text{¢} + 14\text{¢} = 254\text{¢} \). Converting to dollars, this is $2.54.

Problem 7
A full pitcher of orange juice contains about how much juice?
A. 2 ounces     B. 2 liters     C. 2 gallons
Answer: B. 2 liters

Explanation: Using estimation based on standard liquid volume: 2 ounces is about the size of a small shot glass (too small). 2 gallons is very heavy and much larger than a standard household pitcher. 2 liters is the standard size for a beverage pitcher.

Problem 8
Draw a triangle so that two sides are perpendicular. What type of triangle did you draw?
Answer: A right triangle

Explanation: "Perpendicular" means that two lines meet to form a 90° (right) angle. A triangle that contains a 90° angle is called a right triangle.

Problem 9
One fourth of the 48 gems were rubies. How many of the gems were rubies?
Answer: 12 gems

Explanation: To find one-fourth of a number, you divide that number by 4. \( 48 \div 4 = 12 \).

Problem 10
What percent of the gems in problem 9 were not rubies?
Answer: 75%

Explanation: The whole amount of gems represents 100%. If \( \frac{1}{4} \) (which is 25%) are rubies, then the remaining portion is \( 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4} \). The fraction \( \frac{3}{4} \) is equal to 75%.

Problem 11
One thousand fans attended the game, but only 81 fans were pleased with the outcome. What fraction of the fans who attended the game were pleased with the outcome?
Answer: \( \frac{81}{1000} \)

Explanation: A fraction is created by placing the part over the whole. The part is the 81 fans who were pleased, and the whole is the 1000 total fans. This makes the fraction \( \frac{81}{1000} \).

Problem 12
Write the answer to problem 11 as a decimal number. Then use words to name the number.
Answer: 0.081 ; Eighty-one thousandths

Explanation: The fraction \( \frac{81}{1000} \) represents 81 divided by 1000, which moves the decimal point three spaces to the left, yielding 0.081. In word form, because the last digit falls in the thousandths place, it is read as "eighty-one thousandths".

Problem 13
\( 46.01 - (3.68 + 10.2) \)
Answer: 32.13

Explanation: According to the order of operations, perform the addition in parentheses first:
\( 3.68 + 10.20 = 13.88 \)
Next, subtract that sum from 46.01:
\( 46.01 - 13.88 = 32.13 \).

Problem 14
\( 728 + C = 1205 \)
Answer: \( C = 477 \)

Explanation: To find the missing addend \( C \), subtract the known addend from the sum:
\( C = 1205 - 728 \)
\( C = 477 \).

Problem 15
\( 36 \times 10 \)
Answer: 360

Explanation: When multiplying a whole number by 10, you can simply append a zero to the end of the number. \( 36 \times 10 = 360 \).

Problem 16
\( 100 \times 42 \)
Answer: 4,200

Explanation: When multiplying a whole number by 100, you can simply append two zeros to the end of the number. \( 42 \times 100 = 4200 \).

Problem 17
\( \$2.75 \times 1000 \)
Answer: $2,750.00

Explanation: To multiply a decimal by 1000, move the decimal point three places to the right.
\( 2.75 \rightarrow 27.5 \rightarrow 275 \rightarrow 2750 \). Since it is money, we format it as $2,750.00.

Problem 18
\( \$3.17 \times 4 \)
Answer: $12.68

Explanation: Multiply normally as if there were no decimal: \( 317 \times 4 = 1268 \). Then, count the two decimal places from the original problem and place the decimal in the answer: $12.68.

Problem 19
\( 206 \times 5 \)
Answer: 1,030

Explanation: Multiply vertically:
\( 5 \times 6 = 30 \) (Write 0, carry 3)
\( 5 \times 0 = 0 \) (+ 3 carried over = 3)
\( 5 \times 2 = 10 \)
Total = 1030.

Problem 20
\( 37 \times 40 \)
Answer: 1,480

Explanation: Break it down by multiplying \( 37 \times 4 \), then adding a zero at the end.
\( 37 \times 4 = 148 \). Adding the zero makes it 1480.

Problem 21
\( 492 \div 3 \)
Answer: 164

Explanation: Using long division:
3 goes into 4 one time (1), remainder 1.
Bring down 9 to make 19. 3 goes into 19 six times (6), remainder 1.
Bring down 2 to make 12. 3 goes into 12 four times (4), remainder 0.
The quotient is 164.

Problem 22
\( 860 \div 5 \)
Answer: 172

Explanation: Using long division:
5 goes into 8 one time (1), remainder 3.
Bring down 6 to make 36. 5 goes into 36 seven times (7), remainder 1.
Bring down 0 to make 10. 5 goes into 10 two times (2), remainder 0.
The quotient is 172.

Problem 23
\( \$9.30 \div 6 \)
Answer: $1.55

Explanation: Using long division keeping the decimal point aligned:
6 goes into 9 one time (1), remainder 3.
Bring down 3 to make 33. 6 goes into 33 five times (5), remainder 3.
Bring down 0 to make 30. 6 goes into 30 five times (5).
Result: $1.55.

Problem 24
\( 168 \div 2^3 \)
Answer: 21

Explanation: First, evaluate the exponent: \( 2^3 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8 \).
Then divide: \( 168 \div 8 \).
8 goes into 16 two times (2), remainder 0. 8 goes into 8 one time (1).
The quotient is 21.

Problem 25
\( \$20.00 \div 8 \)
Answer: $2.50

Explanation: Using long division:
8 goes into 20 two times (2), remainder 4.
Bring down 0 to make 40. 8 goes into 40 five times (5), remainder 0.
Bring down the last 0. 8 goes into 0 zero times (0).
Keep the decimal in place for currency: $2.50.

Problem 26
\( 1600 \div \sqrt{16} \)
Answer: 400

Explanation: First, evaluate the square root: \( \sqrt{16} = 4 \).
Then perform the division: \( 1600 \div 4 = 400 \).

Problem 27
Find the perimeter and area of this rectangle.
Answer: Perimeter = 32 ft; Area = 60 sq ft

Explanation:
Perimeter is the distance around the outside. A rectangle has two pairs of equal sides: \( 10 + 10 + 6 + 6 = 32\text{ ft} \).
Area is the space inside, calculated as length × width: \( 10 \times 6 = 60\text{ sq ft} \).

Problem 28
Which of these letters has two lines of symmetry?
H A P P Y
Answer: H

Explanation: A line of symmetry divides a shape into two identical mirror-image halves.
- H has a vertical line down the middle and a horizontal line across the middle (2 lines).
- A has only 1 vertical line.
- P has 0 lines.
- Y has only 1 vertical line.

Problem 29
Which angle in this figure looks like it measures about 135°?
Answer: Angle D (or ∠CDA)

Explanation: An angle of 135° is an obtuse angle (greater than 90° but less than 180°). Looking at the trapezoid ABCD:
- Angle A is 90°.
- Angle B is 90°.
- Angle C is acute (less than 90°).
- Angle D is obtuse and clearly wider than a right angle, fitting the 135° description.

Problem 30
Blanca wants to cover a floor with tiles. Which of these shapes of tile will not completely cover the floor (will not tessellate)?
A.
B.
C.
D.
Answer: C. (The regular pentagon)

Explanation: To tessellate (cover a flat surface without leaving gaps), the interior angles of the shape meeting at a vertex must add up to exactly 360°.
- Triangles (60°), squares (90°), and regular hexagons (120°) all divide evenly into 360°.
- A regular pentagon has an interior angle of 108°. Since 360 cannot be evenly divided by 108 (\(360 \div 108 \approx 3.33\)), pentagons leave gaps and cannot tessellate on their own.

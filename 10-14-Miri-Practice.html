1

A group of fish is called a school. There are forty-seven fish in the small school. There are one hundred fifty-six fish in the big school. How many fewer fish are in the small school?

Answer: 109 fewer fish

Solution: Let \(f\) = how many fewer. Equation: \(156 - 47 = f\). Then \(f = 109\).

2

A piece of ribbon that measured 1 yard was cut into two pieces. If one piece was 18 inches long, how many inches long was the other piece?

Answer: 18 inches

Solution: 1 yard = 36 inches. Let \(p\) = the other piece. Equation: \(18 + p = 36\), so \(p = 36 - 18 = 18\) inches.

3

Mrs. Green took fifty-six digital pictures in Hawaii. Her husband took seventy-three digital pictures in Hawaii. Her son took forty-eight digital pictures. In all, how many digital pictures did the Greens take?

Answer: 177 digital pictures

Solution: Let \(t\) = total pictures. Equation: \(56 + 73 + 48 = t\). Then \(t = 129 + 48 = 177\).

Answer: \(5\frac{1}{3}\) (five and one third)

28 Justify

A gardener plans to build a fence around his 20-by-10-foot rectangular vegetable garden. Fencing for the garden can be purchased in 50-foot, 75-foot, or 100-foot rolls. Which roll of fencing should the gardener buy? Explain why.

Answer: The 75-foot roll

Solution: The garden's perimeter is \(2(20 + 10) = 60\) feet. The 50-foot roll is too short. The 75-foot roll is the smallest roll that covers the 60 feet needed (with 15 feet to spare).

29 Estimate

At a gardening center, one pair of gardening gloves costs $18.00, not including a sales tax of 90¢. What is a reasonable estimate of the cost of two pairs of gloves? Explain why your estimate is reasonable.

Answer: About $38

Solution: Two pairs cost \(2 \times \$18.00 = \$36.00\) plus about \(2 \times \$0.90 = \$1.80\) in tax, for an exact total of \$37.80. Rounding up to \$38 is a reasonable estimate because it is close to the exact total and leaves a small cushion.

30 Analyze

How many different three-digit numbers can you write using the digits 7, 4, and 0? Each digit may be used only once, and the digit 0 may not be used in the hundreds place. Label the numbers you write as even or odd.

Answer: 4 numbers: 740 (even), 704 (even), 470 (even), 407 (odd)

Solution: The hundreds digit can only be 7 or 4 (not 0). Listing each once: with 7 first: 740, 704; with 4 first: 470, 407. That is 4 different numbers. Numbers ending in 0 or 4 are even; 407 ends in 7, so it is odd.

Challenge Questions

31 New: terminating decimal

Divide. \(8.4 \div 4\)

Answer: 2.1

Solution: Divide as with whole numbers, placing the decimal point straight up in the quotient: \(4\) goes into \(8\) twice, and into \(4\) tenths once: \(8.4 \div 4 = 2.1\). The decimal terminates (it ends), so 2.1 is exact. Check: \(2.1 \times 4 = 8.4\) ✓

32 New: repeating decimal

Divide. \(10 \div 3\). Write your answer using repeating-decimal notation.

Answer: \(3.\overline{3}\)

Solution: Long division: \(3\) goes into \(10\) three times with remainder \(1\); bringing down a \(0\) gives \(10\) again — the remainder \(1\) repeats forever. So \(10 \div 3 = 3.333\ldots = 3.\overline{3}\). The bar over the 3 shows that the digit 3 repeats without end.

33 New: percent

What is 30% of 90?

Answer: 27

Solution: Change the percent to a fraction: \(30\% = \frac{30}{100} = \frac{3}{10}\). Then \(\frac{3}{10}\) of 90 \(= \frac{3 \times 90}{10} = \frac{270}{10} = 27\).

34 New: PEMDAS

Simplify using the order of operations (PEMDAS).

\(2^3 + (-5) \times 2 - \frac{1}{2} + 0.25\)

Answer: \(-2.25\) (or \(-2\frac{1}{4}\))

Solution: Step 1 — Exponents: \(2^3 = 8\). Step 2 — Multiplication: \((-5) \times 2 = -10\). Now the expression is \(8 + (-10) - \frac{1}{2} + 0.25\). Step 3 — Add/subtract left to right: \(8 - 10 = -2\); \(-2 - \frac{1}{2} = -2.5\); \(-2.5 + 0.25 = -2.25\).

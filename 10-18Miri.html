Represent On 1-cm grid paper, draw a square with sides 5 cm long.

2.(25)
Formulate Write and solve an equation: Wilbur had sixty-seven grapes. Then he ate some grapes. He had thirty-eight grapes left. How many grapes did Wilbur eat?
Answer
\(29\text{ grapes}\)
Solution
Let \(g\) = grapes eaten. Equation: \(67-g=38\) (or \(g+38=67\)). Solve: \(g=67-38=29\).
3.(11, 14)
Formulate Write and solve an equation: The distance from Whery to Radical is 42 km. The distance from Whery to Appletown through Radical is 126 km. How far is it from Radical to Appletown?
Answer
\(84\text{ km}\)
Solution
Let \(d\) = Radical to Appletown. Equation: \(42+d=126\). Solve: \(d=126-42=84\) km.
4.(27)
Raziya arrived home from school at the time shown on the clock and began her homework half an hour later. What time did Raziya begin her homework?
Answer
\(2{:}40\)
Solution
The clock shows 2:10 (minute hand on the 2, hour hand just past the 2). Half an hour later: \(2{:}10+0{:}30=2{:}40\).
5.(3, Inv. 3)
Generalize Write a rule for this sequence and find the next three numbers:
\(1,\ 4,\ 9,\ 16,\ 25,\ 36,\ 49,\) __, __, __, \(\ldots\)
Answer
\(\text{Square numbers; }64,\ 81,\ 100\)
Solution
Each term is a perfect square: \(1^2,2^2,3^2,4^2,5^2,6^2,7^2,\ldots\). The rule is 'square the counting numbers.' Next: \(8^2=64\), \(9^2=81\), \(10^2=100\).

Analyze Use the digits 4, 7, and 8 to write an odd number greater than 500. Each digit may be used only once.
Answer
\(847\)
Solution
An odd number must end in an odd digit — only 7 qualifies, so 7 is last. To be greater than 500, the hundreds digit must be 8 (7 is used). That leaves 4: 847.

29.(Inv. 3, 39)
Joleen has six pieces of wood that she wants to fit together to make a picture frame. Two pieces are 8 inches long, two are 6 inches long, and two are 4 inches long. Using four of the six pieces, how many different rectangular frames could Joleen make? What would be the areas of the rectangles formed?
Answer
\(3\text{ frames: }48,\ 32,\ 24\text{ in.}^2\)
Solution
A rectangle needs two matching pairs. From the pairs \\(\\{8,8\\},\\{6,6\\},\\{4,4\\}\\), choose any two: \(\\binom{3}{2}=3\) different frames.
• 8 in. × 6 in. → area \(48\) in.\\(^2\\)
• 8 in. × 4 in. → area \(32\) in.\\(^2\\)
• 6 in. × 4 in. → area \(24\) in.\\(^2\\)
30.(42)
Estimate Each of 4 school buses can carry 52 passengers. What is a reasonable estimate of the total number of passengers the four buses can carry? Explain why your estimate is reasonable.
Answer
\(200\text{ passengers}\)
Solution
Round 52 to the compatible number 50: \(4\times 50=200\). This is reasonable because 52 is close to 50, and \(4\times 50\) is easy to compute mentally. (Exact: \(4\times 52=208\).)
RWC.Real-World Connection
The zoo's insect house has 35 glass cases. An average of 17 crickets live in 22 of the cases and an average of 15 grasshoppers live in 13 of the cases. What is a reasonable estimate of the total number of insects that live in the glass cases at the zoo? Explain why your answer is reasonable.
Answer
\(\approx 600\text{ insects}\)
Solution
Use compatible numbers: round 17 crickets to 20 → \(22\times 20=440\) crickets. Round 13 cases to 10 → \(10\times 15=150\) grasshoppers. Total \(\approx 440+150=590\approx 600\) insects. This is reasonable because the rounded numbers are close to the actual values and easy to multiply mentally.

Terms & People to Know
  • Repeal — a vote to end a law
  • Boycott — an organized campaign where a group of individuals refuse to buy a certain product
  • Proclamation — an important official announcement, usually made to the public
  • Stamp Act — a tax on all paper goods
  • Tea Act of 1773 — lowered the price of tea but kept the tax on tea; caused the Boston Tea Party
  • Parliament — lawmaking body of England
  • Loyalists — people who supported Great Britain
  • Common Sense — a pamphlet by Thomas Paine that helped colonists choose independence
  • Minutemen — American colonists ready to fight the British at a minute's notice
  • George Washington — served as leader of the Continental army
  • Molly Pitcher (Mary Hayes) — a woman who helped very thirsty men survive battles
  • John Paul Jones — captain of the Bonhomme Richard; known as the father of the American Navy
  • Nathan Hale — gave a speech to inspire colonists to keep fighting for independence
  • Crispus Attucks — known as the first casualty of the American Revolutionary War
French and Indian War (1756–1763)
  • British fought against the French and Indians for French territory
  • William Pitt believed the British needed 3 things to win the war:
    • Money, lots of troops, and control of the Niagara River and the St. Lawrence River
Rights of the Englishmen
  • Homes could not be searched without a warrant
  • Right to a speedy trial by jury
  • Right to vote
  • Right to private property
  • Right to gather peaceably to talk about a problem

No Taxation without Representation — the colonists' argument: they should not be taxed without a say in government.

Road to Revolution
  • Townshend Act — taxes on goods sent to the colonies from Britain (paint, tea, glass, paper goods, lead)
  • Boston Tea Party (Dec 16, 1773) — caused by the Tea Act of 1773; patriots dressed as Mohawk Indians dumped 342 chests of tea into Boston Harbor
  • Intolerable Acts — 5 laws passed to punish the colonists of Boston for destroying the British tea
  • First Continental Congress — first meeting of colonial representatives (Georgia was not present); voted to cut off all trade with Britain (boycott) until the Intolerable Acts were repealed; issued a declaration of rights; sent petitions to King George III; agreed to meet again if things did not improve
Declaration of Independence
  • Written by Thomas Jefferson
  • Stated 3 unalienable rights: Life, Liberty, and the Pursuit of Happiness
  • Signed July 4, 1776
Foreign Help During the War
  • France — sent troops, money, supplies, and a navy
  • Poland — built forts and defenses for troops
  • Germany — trained troops without pay
  • Spain and Netherlands — joined the fight against Britain
Key Battles & Events
  • Battle of Saratoga — Britain wanted to capture Saratoga to cut off New England; it finally convinced France to join America as an ally
  • Valley Forge — Washington's army spent a very difficult winter here; extremely cold with no supplies
  • Battle of Yorktown — final major battle of the American Revolution
  • Treaty of Paris — officially ended the Revolutionary War

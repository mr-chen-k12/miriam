Lesson Practice

6.RepresentUse words to write \(2{,}700{,}000\).
Answer: two million, seven hundred thousand
The digit \(2\) is in the millions place and \(7\) is in the hundred-thousands place, so \(2{,}700{,}000\) = two million, seven hundred thousand.
7.RepresentUse digits to write eighty-two thousand, five hundred.
Answer: \(82{,}500\)
“Eighty-two thousand” = \(82{,}000\) and “five hundred” = \(500\). \(82{,}000 + 500 = 82{,}500\).
8.Each day, classes at Kennedy Elementary School end 4 hours 20 minutes later than the time shown on the clock. What time do classes end each day?
The clock shows 10:40.
Answer: 3:00
The clock shows 10:40. Add 4 hours: \(10{:}40 + 4\text{ hr} = 2{:}40\). Add 20 minutes: \(2{:}40 + 20\text{ min} = 3{:}00\).
9.ConnectChange this addition problem to a multiplication problem: \[4+4+4+4+4+4+4+4\]
Answer: \(8 \times 4 = 32\)
There are eight 4's being added. Repeated addition of the same number is multiplication: \(8 \times 4 = 32\).
10.a. Round \(176\) to the nearest ten.
b. Round \(\$17.60\) to the nearest dollar.
Answer: a. \(180\)   b. \(\$18\)
a. The ones digit of \(176\) is \(6 \ge 5\), so round the tens place up: \(176 \to 180\).
b. \(\$17.60\) has \(60¢ \ge 50¢\), so round up to the next whole dollar: \(\$18\).
11.RepresentThe number represented by point \(X\) is how much less than the number represented by point \(Y\)?
Answer: \(100\) less
Each tick mark stands for \(20\). Point \(X\) is one tick before \(600\), so \(X = 580\). Point \(Y\) is one tick before \(700\), so \(Y = 680\). \(680 - 580 = 100\).
12.Multiply: a. \(2 \times 8\)   b. \(5 \times 6\)   c. \(4 \times 5\)   d. \(5 \times 8\)
Answer: a. \(16\)   b. \(30\)   c. \(20\)   d. \(40\)
\(2 \times 8 = 16\), \(5 \times 6 = 30\), \(4 \times 5 = 20\), \(5 \times 8 = 40\).
13.a. \(3 \times 3\)   b. \(5 \times 5\)   c. \(9 \times 9\)   d. \(10 \times 10\)
Answer: a. \(9\)   b. \(25\)   c. \(81\)   d. \(100\)
\(3 \times 3 = 9\), \(5 \times 5 = 25\), \(9 \times 9 = 81\), \(10 \times 10 = 100\).
14.a. \(9 \times 7\)   b. \(9 \times 4\)   c. \(9 \times 8\)   d. \(9 \times 12\)
Answer: a. \(63\)   b. \(36\)   c. \(72\)   d. \(108\)
\(9 \times 7 = 63\), \(9 \times 4 = 36\), \(9 \times 8 = 72\), \(9 \times 12 = 108\).
15.\[\sqrt{36}+\sqrt{49}\]
Answer: \(13\)
\(\sqrt{36} = 6\) and \(\sqrt{49} = 7\), so \(6 + 7 = 13\).
c.Fifty pennies are what fraction of a dollar? Write the value of 50 pennies using a dollar sign and a decimal point.
Answer: \(\frac{1}{2}\) of a dollar; \(\$0.50\)
50 pennies = 50 cents out of 100 cents = \(\frac{50}{100} = \frac{1}{2}\) of a dollar, which is written \(\$0.50\).
d.Compare: \(\frac{1}{10}\) of a dollar ○ \(\frac{1}{4}\) of a dollar
Answer: \(\frac{1}{10} < \frac{1}{4}\)
\(\frac{1}{10}\) of a dollar = \(\$0.10\) and \(\frac{1}{4}\) of a dollar = \(\$0.25\). Since \(\$0.10 < \$0.25\), \(\frac{1}{10} < \frac{1}{4}\).
e.Compare: \(\frac{1}{2}\) of a dollar ○ \(\$0.25\)
Answer: \(\frac{1}{2} > \$0.25\)
\(\frac{1}{2}\) of a dollar = \(\$0.50\), and \(\$0.50 > \$0.25\).
f.Look at these coins: a nickel (5¢), a quarter (25¢), a dime (10¢), and a half-dollar (50¢). List all of the different ways to pair two coins.
Answer: 6 pairs — nickel+quarter (30¢), nickel+dime (15¢), nickel+half-dollar (55¢), quarter+dime (35¢), quarter+half-dollar (75¢), dime+half-dollar (60¢)
With 4 coins, the number of ways to choose 2 is \(\frac{4 \times 3}{2} = 6\). The pairs are: (5¢, 25¢), (5¢, 10¢), (5¢, 50¢), (25¢, 10¢), (25¢, 50¢), (10¢, 50¢).

Written Practice

Formulate — Write and solve equations for problems 1–3.

1.Quinh is 49 inches tall. His dad is 70 inches tall. Quinh is how many inches shorter than his dad?
Answer: \(21\) inches shorter
\(70 - 49 = 21\). Quinh is 21 inches shorter than his dad.
2.Smith went into the store with \(\$36.49\). He bought a book and left the store with \(\$11.80\). How much money did Smith spend in the store?
Answer: \(\$24.69\)
\(\$36.49 - \$11.80 = \$24.69\). Smith spent \(\$24.69\).
3.Beth answered eleven of the twenty-five questions at school. She answered the rest of the questions as homework. How many questions did Beth answer as homework?
Answer: \(14\) questions
\(25 - 11 = 14\). Beth answered 14 questions as homework.
4.Write the number of shaded rectangles shown as a mixed number.
Answer: \(3\frac{1}{6}\)
Three whole rectangles are shaded, and the fourth rectangle is divided into 6 equal parts with 1 part shaded, so the mixed number is \(3\frac{1}{6}\).
5.VerifyWhich letter below appears to have no right angles?
THEN
Answer: N
T, H, and E are all made with horizontal and vertical segments that meet at right angles. N has a diagonal segment, so it has no right angles.

24.Which digit in \(8{,}367{,}254\) is in the ten-thousands place?
Answer: \(6\)
Reading places from the right: ones \(4\), tens \(5\), hundreds \(2\), thousands \(7\), ten-thousands \(6\), hundred-thousands \(3\), millions \(8\).
25.Multiple ChoiceWhich of the money amounts below does not equal one half of a dollar?
A 2 quarters    B 0.50¢    C $0.50    D 50¢
Answer: B
2 quarters = 50¢, \(\$0.50\) = 50¢, and 50¢ all equal half a dollar. But \(0.50¢\) means half of one cent (\(\$0.005\)), which is not half a dollar.
26.Multiple ChoiceIf a rectangle is 5 in. long and 4 in. wide, then its area is _______.
A 9 in.    B 18 in.    C 20 sq. in.    D 18 sq. in.
Answer: C
Area of a rectangle = length × width = \(5 \times 4 = 20\) square inches.
27.Compare: a. \(-12\) ○ \(-21\)    b. \(\frac{1}{4}\) of a dollar ○ \(\$0.25\)
Answer: a. \(-12 > -21\)   b. \(=\)
a. On the number line \(-12\) is to the right of \(-21\), so \(-12 > -21\).
b. \(\frac{1}{4}\) of a dollar = \(\$0.25\), so they are equal.
28.PredictWrite the tenth term of each pattern below:
a. \(12, 24, 36, 48, 60, \dots\)
b. \(11, 22, 33, 44, 55, \dots\)
Answer: a. \(120\)   b. \(110\)
a. The pattern counts by 12's (multiples of 12). The \(n\)th term is \(12n\), so the 10th term is \(12 \times 10 = 120\).
b. The pattern counts by 11's. The 10th term is \(11 \times 10 = 110\).
29.Look at these bills: a \(\$1\) bill, a \(\$5\) bill, a \(\$10\) bill, and a \(\$20\) bill. List all of the different ways to pair two bills.
Answer: 6 pairs — ($1,$5)=$6, ($1,$10)=$11, ($1,$20)=$21, ($5,$10)=$15, ($5,$20)=$25, ($10,$20)=$30
Choosing 2 bills from 4 gives \(\frac{4 \times 3}{2} = 6\) different pairs.
30.EstimateThe state of Louisiana has 397 miles of coastline. The state of Oregon has 296 miles of coastline. What is a reasonable estimate of the combined length of those coastlines? Explain why your estimate is reasonable.
Answer: About \(700\) miles
Round each to the nearest hundred: \(397 \approx 400\) and \(296 \approx 300\). \(400 + 300 = 700\). This is reasonable because both numbers were rounded to the nearest hundred, and \(397 + 296 = 693\), which is close to \(700\).

Real-World Connection

Maria had a quarter, a dime, and a nickel in her pocket. How much money did Maria have in her pocket?
a. Write the amount as a fraction of a dollar.
b. Write the value of the coins using a dollar sign and a decimal point.
c. Compare the amount to \(\frac{1}{2}\) of a dollar.
Answer: a. \(\frac{2}{5}\) of a dollar   b. \(\$0.40\)   c. \(\$0.40 < \frac{1}{2}\) of a dollar
Quarter + dime + nickel = \(25¢ + 10¢ + 5¢ = 40¢\).
a. \(\frac{40}{100} = \frac{2}{5}\) of a dollar.
b. \(\$0.40\).
c. \(\$0.40 < \$0.50\), so it is less than \(\frac{1}{2}\) of a dollar.

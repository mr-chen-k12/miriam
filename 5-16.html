Math Assignment Solutions (1-30)

1. How many baskets did Joe make in the first game?
Answer: 4 baskets
Looking at the "Baskets Made by Joe" line graph, the point corresponding to Game 1 aligns with 4 on the vertical axis (Number of Baskets).
2. Each basket is worth 2 points. How many points did Joe score in Game 5?
Answer: 14 points
According to the graph, Joe made 7 baskets in Game 5. Multiplying the number of baskets by the points per basket gives $7 \times 2 = 14$ points.
3. How many more points did Joe score in Game 5 than in Game 3?
Answer: 2 points
In Game 5, he made 7 baskets ($7 \times 2 = 14$ points). In Game 3, he made 6 baskets ($6 \times 2 = 12$ points). The difference is $14 - 12 = 2$ points.
4. The 3-pound melon cost $1.44. What was the cost per pound?
Answer: $0.48 or 48¢
Divide the total cost by the number of pounds: $\$1.44 \div 3 = \$0.48$.
5. Jim spun all the way around in the air and dunked the basketball. Jim turned about how many degrees?
Answer: 360°
A complete circle or a full spin "all the way around" is exactly 360 degrees.
6. Sammy bought a pair of shoes priced at $47.99. The sales tax was $2.88. Sammy gave the clerk $60.00. How much money should he get back?
Answer: $9.13
First, find the total cost by adding the price and the tax: $\$47.99 + \$2.88 = \$50.87$. Then subtract the total cost from the amount Sammy paid: $\$60.00 - \$50.87 = \$9.13$.
7. If the perimeter of a square is 1 foot, how many inches long is each side?
Answer: 3 inches
First, convert the perimeter into inches. 1 foot = 12 inches. Since a square has 4 sides of equal length, divide the total perimeter by 4: $12 \div 4 = 3$ inches per side.
8. A dollar bill weighs about 1 gram. Use this information to estimate the number of dollar bills it would take to weigh 1 kilogram.
Answer: 1000 dollar bills
The prefix "kilo-" means 1,000. Therefore, 1 kilogram is equal to 1,000 grams. If one bill weighs 1 gram, it takes 1,000 bills to reach 1,000 grams.
9. One fourth of the 64 clowns had red noses. How many clowns had red noses? Draw a picture to illustrate the problem.
Answer: 16 clowns
To find one fourth of a number, divide the number by 4. $64 \div 4 = 16$. (A drawing would show 64 objects separated into 4 equal groups, with 16 objects in each group).
10. What percent of the clowns in problem 9 did not have red noses?
Answer: 75%
If $\frac{1}{4}$ of the clowns *did* have red noses, then the remaining $\frac{3}{4}$ did *not*. As a percentage, $\frac{3}{4}$ translates to $75\%$.
11. Kerry knew that her trip would take about 7 hours. If she left at half past nine in the morning, around what time should she arrive?
Answer: 4:30 PM
"Half past nine in the morning" is 9:30 AM. Counting forward 7 hours from 9:30 AM lands you exactly on 4:30 PM.
12. Jill's boat holds 42 containers. Each container can hold 8 big fish. How many big fish can Jill put in her 42 containers?
Answer: 336 big fish
Multiply the number of containers by the number of fish each container holds: $42 \times 8 = 336$.
13. Eighty-eight horseshoes are enough to shoe how many horses?
Answer: 22 horses
Since each horse requires 4 horseshoes, you divide the total number of horseshoes by 4: $88 \div 4 = 22$.
14. Triangles ABC and DEF are congruent. Which transformations would move $\triangle ABC$ to the same position as $\triangle DEF$?
Answer: Reflection
Looking at the figures, $\triangle ABC$ faces to the right, while $\triangle DEF$ is a mirror image facing left. The transformation that achieves a mirror image flip is called a reflection.
15. Which of these words does not describe the triangles in problem 14? A. similar B. obtuse C. scalene D. isosceles
Answer: D. isosceles
The triangles have no equal sides (scalene) and one angle greater than 90° (obtuse). Because they are congruent, they are also mathematically similar. They do not have two sides of equal length, so they are *not* isosceles.
16. $0.625 - (0.5 + 0.12)$
Answer: 0.005
By order of operations, evaluate the parentheses first: $0.5 + 0.12 = 0.62$. Then perform the subtraction: $0.625 - 0.62 = 0.005$.
17. $47 \times 100$
Answer: 4700
When multiplying a whole number by 100, you simply append two zeros to the end of the number.
18. $328 \times 4$
Answer: 1312
$4 \times 8 = 32$. Write 2, carry 3.
$4 \times 2 = 8$. $8 + 3 = 11$. Write 1, carry 1.
$4 \times 3 = 12$. $12 + 1 = 13$. Result is 1312.
19. $43 \times 32$
Answer: 1376
$43 \times 2 = 86$.
$43 \times 30 = 1290$.
Adding the partial products: $86 + 1290 = 1376$.
20. $25 \times 35$
Answer: 875
$25 \times 5 = 125$.
$25 \times 30 = 750$.
Adding the partial products: $125 + 750 = 875$.
21. $5 \overline{) 4317}$
Answer: 863 R 2
5 goes into 43 eight times (40), remainder 3. Bring down 1 (31). 5 goes into 31 six times (30), remainder 1. Bring down 7 (17). 5 goes into 17 three times (15), remainder 2. The quotient is 863 with a remainder of 2.
22. $8 \overline{) \$40.00}$
Answer: $5.00
Since 8 times 5 is 40, 8 divides evenly into 40 exactly 5 times. Keep the decimal in the same place.
23. $6 \overline{) 3963}$
Answer: 660 R 3
6 goes into 39 six times (36), remainder 3. Bring down 6 (36). 6 goes into 36 six times (36), remainder 0. Bring down 3. 6 goes into 3 zero times. You must record this zero! The remainder is 3.
24. $426 \div 3$
Answer: 142
3 goes into 4 one time, remainder 1. Bring down 2 (12). 3 goes into 12 four times (12), remainder 0. Bring down 6. 3 goes into 6 two times (6), remainder 0.
25. $2524 \div 4$
Answer: 631
4 goes into 25 six times (24), remainder 1. Bring down 2 (12). 4 goes into 12 three times (12), remainder 0. Bring down 4. 4 goes into 4 one time (4).
26. $60 \times 700$
Answer: 42000
Multiply the non-zero digits first: $6 \times 7 = 42$. Then, count the total number of zeros in the problem (one in 60, two in 700 = three zeros) and append them to 42.
27. Draw and shade circles to show that $2 \frac{1}{2}$ equals $\frac{5}{2}$.
Answer: Three total circles. Shade two entirely, and one halfway.
If you draw 3 circles and cut each in half, you have 6 halves. To show $2 \frac{1}{2}$, you fully shade 2 circles (which covers 4 halves), and shade 1 half of the final circle. Counting up the shaded halves proves it equals $\frac{5}{2}$.
28. $4 + 3 + 27 + 35 + 8 + N = 112$
Answer: N = 35
First, add all the known numbers on the left side: $4 + 3 = 7$; $7 + 27 = 34$; $34 + 35 = 69$; $69 + 8 = 77$.
Now the equation is $77 + N = 112$. Subtract 77 from 112 to solve for N: $112 - 77 = 35$.
29. Segment BC is 1.7 cm long. How many centimeters long is segment AB?
Answer: 1.8 cm
Looking at the ruler, point A starts at $1.0\text{ cm}$ and point C ends at $4.5\text{ cm}$. This means the entire segment $AC = 4.5 - 1.0 = 3.5\text{ cm}$.
If $BC = 1.7\text{ cm}$, subtract that from the total to find $AB$: $3.5\text{ cm} - 1.7\text{ cm} = 1.8\text{ cm}$.
30. Write a decimal addition problem that is illustrated by the lengths of segments AB, BC, and AC in problem 29.
Answer: 1.8 + 1.7 = 3.5
The length of segment AB plus the length of segment BC equals the total length AC. Putting in the decimal values we found: $1.8\text{ cm} + 1.7\text{ cm} = 3.5\text{ cm}$.

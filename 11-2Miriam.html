Problem 1 Formulate
Each of the 3 boats carried 12 people. In all, how many people were in the 3 boats? Write and solve an equation.
We multiply the number of boats by the number of people on each boat: \[ 3 \times 12 = n \] \[ n = 36 \] 36 people
Problem 2 Formulate
The book cost $6.98. The tax was 42¢. What was the total price? Write and solve an equation.
First write the tax as dollars: \(42\text{¢} = \$0.42\). Then add: \[ \$6.98 + \$0.42 = \$7.40 \] \$7.40
Problem 3 Formulate
Claire read six hundred twenty minutes for an afterschool reading program. Ashanti read four hundred seventeen minutes. Claire read how many more minutes than Ashanti? Write and solve an equation.
Write the numbers in digits: Claire read \(620\) minutes and Ashanti read \(417\) minutes. Subtract to compare: \[ 620 - 417 = 203 \] 203 minutes
Problem 4 Connect
Use the numbers 4, 12, and 48 to write two multiplication facts and two division facts.
The three numbers form a fact family: \[ 4 \times 12 = 48 \qquad 12 \times 4 = 48 \] \[ 48 \div 4 = 12 \qquad 48 \div 12 = 4 \] 4 × 12 = 48,   12 × 4 = 48,   48 ÷ 4 = 12,   48 ÷ 12 = 4
Problem 5
Justin ran the perimeter of the block. How far did Justin run? The measurements of the block are shown on the figure below.
The perimeter is the distance around the block. Add all four sides: \[ 100 + 50 + 100 + 50 = 300 \] 300 yards
Problem 6
Justin ran around the block in 58.7 seconds. Write “58.7” with words.
The digit 7 is in the tenths place, so we read the decimal part as “seven tenths”: fifty-eight and seven tenths
Problem 7 Represent
Use digits to write twelve million, seven hundred fifty thousand. Which digit is in the hundred-thousands place?
Writing the number in digits: \[ 12{,}750{,}000 \] Counting places from the right — ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands, hundred-thousands — the hundred-thousands digit is the 7 in 750,000.
12,750,000; the digit is 7
Problem 8 Estimate
Round 783 and 217 to the nearest hundred. Then subtract the smaller rounded number from the larger rounded number.
783 rounds up to \(800\) (the tens digit is 8), and 217 rounds down to \(200\) (the tens digit is 1). Then subtract: \[ 800 - 200 = 600 \] 600
Problem 9
The time shown on the clock is an evening time. Alyssa’s school day begins 9 hours 30 minutes later than that time. What time does Alyssa’s school day begin?
The clock shows 10:35 p.m. (the minute hand points to 35 minutes, and the hour hand is between 10 and 11). \[ 10{:}35 \text{ p.m.} + 9 \text{ hours} = 7{:}35 \text{ a.m.} \] \[ 7{:}35 \text{ a.m.} + 30 \text{ minutes} = 8{:}05 \text{ a.m.} \]
8:05 a.m.
8:05 a.m.
Problem 10 Connect
Write this addition problem as a multiplication problem: \[ \$3.75 + \$3.75 + \$3.75 + \$3.75 \]
The same amount, $3.75, is added four times. Adding the same number over and over is multiplication: \[ 4 \times \$3.75 = \$15.00 \] 4 × \$3.75  (= \$15.00)

Problem 19
\( \$5.00 - \$4.29 \)
Regroup across the zeros: \( \$5.00 = \$4.99 + 1\text{¢} \). Then subtract: \(99\text{¢} - 29\text{¢} = 70\text{¢}\), and add back the extra cent: \[ \$5.00 - \$4.29 = \$0.71 \] \$0.71

Problem 21
Find \(n\):   \( \begin{align} n \\ -\ 27.9 \\ \hline 48.4 \end{align} \)
To undo the subtraction, add the two known numbers: \[ n = 48.4 + 27.9 = 76.3 \] Check: \(76.3 - 27.9 = 48.4\) ✓
n = 76.3
Problem 22
Find \(c\):   \( \begin{align} 46.2 \\ +\quad c \\ \hline 52.9 \end{align} \)
To find the missing addend, subtract the known addend from the sum: \[ c = 52.9 - 46.2 = 6.7 \] Check: \(46.2 + 6.7 = 52.9\) ✓
c = 6.7

Problem 25
The length of segment \(AB\) is 5 cm. The length of segment \(BC\) is 4 cm. What is the length of segment \(AC\)?
Point \(B\) lies between \(A\) and \(C\), so the lengths of the two parts add up to the whole segment: \[ AC = AB + BC = 5 \text{ cm} + 4 \text{ cm} = 9 \text{ cm} \] 9 cm
Problem 26 Estimate
Draw and shade circles to show the number \(3\dfrac{3}{8}\).
The whole number 3 tells us to shade 3 whole circles. The fraction \(\dfrac{3}{8}\) tells us to shade 3 of the 8 equal parts of one more circle:
3 whole circles shaded, plus 3 of 8 parts of a fourth circle

Problem 28 Multiple Choice
Which of the following is more than one second but less than two seconds?
A  0.15 sec
B  1.5 sec
C  2.1 sec
D  2.15 sec
We need a time \(t\) with \(1 < t < 2\). Choice A (0.15 sec) is less than one second. Choices C (2.1 sec) and D (2.15 sec) are both more than two seconds. Only 1.5 sec falls between one and two seconds: B  (1.5 sec)
Problem 29
Write these numbers in order from least to greatest: \[ 250{,}000 \qquad 47{,}000 \qquad 9000 \qquad 3{,}100{,}000 \qquad 600 \]
Compare by the number of digits first, then by the digits themselves: \(600\) has 3 digits; \(9{,}000\) has 4; \(47{,}000\) has 5; \(250{,}000\) has 6; \(3{,}100{,}000\) has 7. 600;   9,000;   47,000;   250,000;   3,100,000
Problem 30
These thermometers show the average daily minimum and maximum temperatures in New York City’s Central Park during the month of July. What is the difference in degrees between the two temperatures?
Minimum
Maximum
Each small tick stands for 2 degrees. The minimum thermometer’s liquid stops halfway between 68 and 70, at \(69^\circ\text{F}\). The maximum thermometer’s liquid stops halfway between 82 and 84, at \(83^\circ\text{F}\). Subtract to find the difference: \[ 83 - 69 = 14 \] 14 degrees (69°F minimum, 83°F maximum)

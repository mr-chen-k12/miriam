Problem 1 Formulate
Each of the 4 vans carried 14 students. In all, how many students were in the 4 vans? Write and solve an equation.
We multiply the number of vans by the number of students in each van: \[ 4 \times 14 = n \] \[ n = 56 \] 56 students
Problem 2 Formulate
The notebook cost $5.85. The tax was 38¢. What was the total price? Write and solve an equation.
First write the tax as dollars: \(38\text{¢} = \$0.38\). Then add: \[ \$5.85 + \$0.38 = \$6.23 \] \$6.23
Problem 3 Formulate
Mateo collected seven hundred forty baseball cards. Priya collected five hundred eighteen baseball cards. Mateo collected how many more baseball cards than Priya? Write and solve an equation.
Write the numbers in digits: Mateo collected \(740\) cards and Priya collected \(518\) cards. Subtract to compare: \[ 740 - 518 = 222 \] 222 baseball cards
Problem 4 Connect
Use the numbers 6, 11, and 66 to write two multiplication facts and two division facts.
The three numbers form a fact family: \[ 6 \times 11 = 66 \qquad 11 \times 6 = 66 \] \[ 66 \div 6 = 11 \qquad 66 \div 11 = 6 \] 6 × 11 = 66,   11 × 6 = 66,   66 ÷ 6 = 11,   66 ÷ 11 = 6

Problem 7 Represent
Use digits to write fifteen million, four hundred eighty thousand. Which digit is in the hundred-thousands place?

Problem 25
The length of segment \(PQ\) is 6 cm. The length of segment \(QR\) is 5 cm. What is the length of segment \(PR\)?
Point \(Q\) lies between \(P\) and \(R\), so the lengths of the two parts add up to the whole segment: \[ PR = PQ + QR = 6 \text{ cm} + 5 \text{ cm} = 11 \text{ cm} \] 11 cm

Problem 28 Multiple Choice
Which of the following is more than two seconds but less than three seconds?
A  0.25 sec
B  1.85 sec
C  2.4 sec
D  3.2 sec
We need a time \(t\) with \(2 < t < 3\). Choice A (0.25 sec) and Choice B (1.85 sec) are both less than two seconds. Choice D (3.2 sec) is more than three seconds. Only 2.4 sec falls between two and three seconds: C  (2.4 sec)

*29. (32) PREDICT

Write the twelfth term of each pattern below:

a. \(11, 22, 33, 44, \ldots\)

b. \(12, 24, 36, 48, \ldots\)

Answer: a. \(132\)    b. \(144\).

Solution: a. Each term is a multiple of 11: the \(n\)th term is \(11n\). The twelfth term is \(11 \times 12 = 132\). b. Each term is a multiple of 12: the \(n\)th term is \(12n\). The twelfth term is \(12 \times 12 = 144\).

*30. (23)

Name a real-world example of

a. parallel lines.

b. perpendicular lines.

Answer: a. Railroad tracks (answers may vary). b. The corner where a wall meets the floor (answers may vary).

Solution: a. Parallel lines never meet — for example, the two rails of a railroad track, the opposite edges of a ruler, or the lane lines on a highway. b. Perpendicular lines meet at a right (\(90^\circ\)) angle — for example, where a wall meets the floor, the corner of a door frame, or the lines of a plus sign \(+\).

Real-World Connection

The school choir is having a car wash to raise money to buy new songbooks. Each car wash will cost \(350\text{¢}\).

a.

Write this money amount using a dollar sign and a decimal point.

Answer: \(\$3.50\).

Solution: \(350\) cents \(= \frac{350}{100}\) dollars \(= 3.50\) dollars, written \(\$3.50\).

b.

Draw and shade circles to represent \(3\frac{1}{2}\) as a mixed number.

Answer: Draw 4 circles; shade 3 completely and shade exactly half of the fourth circle.

Solution: The whole number 3 means 3 fully shaded circles. The fraction \(\frac{1}{2}\) means one more circle with exactly half of it shaded. Altogether the shaded part represents \(3\frac{1}{2}\).

c.

Use words to write \(3\frac{50}{100}\).

Answer: Three and fifty hundredths.

Solution: The whole-number part 3 is "three," and \(\frac{50}{100}\) is read as "fifty hundredths" (the second decimal place is the hundredths place).

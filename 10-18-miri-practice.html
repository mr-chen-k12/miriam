2.(25)
Formulate Write and solve an equation: Marta had eighty-four stickers. Then she gave away some stickers. She had fifty-seven stickers left. How many stickers did Marta give away?
Answer
\(27\text{ stickers}\)
Solution
Let \(s\) = stickers given away. Equation: \(84-s=57\) (or \(s+57=84\)). Solve: \(s=84-57=27\).
3.(11, 14)
Formulate Write and solve an equation: The distance from Greenville to Milltown is 35 km. The distance from Greenville to Lakeview through Milltown is 112 km. How far is it from Milltown to Lakeview?
Answer
\(77\text{ km}\)
Solution
Let \(d\) = Milltown to Lakeview. Equation: \(35+d=112\). Solve: \(d=112-35=77\) km.

Generalize Write a rule for this sequence and find the next three numbers:
\(2,\ 4,\ 8,\ 16,\ 32,\) __, __, __, \(\ldots\)
Answer
\(\text{Multiply by 2 each time; }64,\ 128,\ 256\)
Solution
Each term is twice the one before it: \(2\times 2=4\), \(4\times 2=8\), and so on. Next: \(32\times 2=64\), \(64\times 2=128\), \(128\times 2=256\).

29.(Inv. 3, 39)
Priya has six pieces of wood to make a picture frame. Two pieces are 10 inches long, two are 7 inches long, and two are 5 inches long. Using four of the six pieces, how many different rectangular frames could Priya make? What would be the areas of the rectangles formed?
Answer
\(3\text{ frames: }70,\ 50,\ 35\text{ in.}^2\)
Solution
A rectangle needs two matching pairs. From the pairs \(\{10,10\},\{7,7\},\{5,5\}\), choose any two: \(\binom{3}{2}=3\) different frames.
• 10 in. × 7 in. → area \(70\) in.\(^2\)
• 10 in. × 5 in. → area \(50\) in.\(^2\)
• 7 in. × 5 in. → area \(35\) in.\(^2\)
30.(42)
Estimate Each of 6 vans can carry 38 passengers. What is a reasonable estimate of the total number of passengers the six vans can carry? Explain why your estimate is reasonable.
Answer
\(240\text{ passengers}\)
Solution
Round 38 to the compatible number 40: \(6\times 40=240\). This is reasonable because 38 is close to 40, and \(6\times 40\) is easy to compute mentally. (Exact: \(6\times 38=228\).)
RWC.Real-World Connection
The library has 30 shelves. An average of 24 books sit on 18 of the shelves and an average of 31 books sit on 12 of the shelves. What is a reasonable estimate of the total number of books on the shelves? Explain why your answer is reasonable.
Answer
\(\approx 800\text{ books}\)
Solution
Use compatible numbers: round 24 books to 25 → \(18\times 25=450\) books. Round 31 books to 30 and 12 shelves to 10 → \(10\times 30=300\) books. Total \(\approx 450+300=750\approx 800\) books. This is reasonable because the rounded numbers are close to the actual values and easy to multiply mentally.

32.(new)

Solution

34.(new)
Evaluate using the order of operations: \(3^2+(-4)\times 1.5-\dfrac14+0.5\)
Answer
\(3.25\)
Solution
First the exponent: \(3^2=9\). Then multiplication: \((-4)\times 1.5=-6\). Now add and subtract left to right: \(9+(-6)=3\); \(3-\dfrac14=3-0.25=2.75\); \(2.75+0.5=3.25\).

Problem 1
Three quarters, 6 dimes, 3 nickels, and 9 pennies is how much money?
Answer: \$1.59
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • Find the value of the quarters: \( 3 \times 25\text{¢} = 75\text{¢} \)
  • Find the value of the dimes: \( 6 \times 10\text{¢} = 60\text{¢} \)
  • Find the value of the nickels: \( 3 \times 5\text{¢} = 15\text{¢} \)
  • Find the value of the pennies: \( 9 \times 1\text{¢} = 9\text{¢} \)
  • Add the amounts together:
    $$75\text{¢} + 60\text{¢} + 15\text{¢} + 9\text{¢} = 159\text{¢}$$
  • Convert to dollars: \( 159\text{¢} = \$1.59 \)
Problem 2
Nia put 50 math books as evenly as possible in 7 stacks.
a. How many stacks had exactly 7 books?
b. How many stacks had 8 books?
Answer: a. 6 stacks  |  b. 1 stack
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • Divide the total number of books by the number of stacks:
    $$50 \div 7 = 7 \text{ with a remainder of } 1$$
  • This means if you put 7 books in each of the 7 stacks, you use \( 7 \times 7 = 49 \) books.
  • You have 1 book left over, so you add it to one of the stacks to distribute as evenly as possible.
  • Therefore, 6 stacks will have exactly 7 books, and 1 stack will have \( 7 + 1 = 8 \) books.
Problem 3
Martin paid one dollar for a folder and received 43¢ back in change. How much did the folder cost?
Answer: 57¢ or \$0.57
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • Subtract the change received from the amount paid (1 dollar = 100¢):
    $$100\text{¢} - 43\text{¢} = 57\text{¢}$$

Problem 5
Round 6348 to the nearest thousand. Round 1863 to the nearest thousand. Find the sum of the two rounded numbers.
Answer: 8,000
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • Round 6348: The hundreds digit is 3 (less than 5), so it rounds down to 6,000.
  • Round 1863: The hundreds digit is 8 (5 or greater), so it rounds up to 2,000.
  • Add the two rounded numbers:
    $$6,000 + 2,000 = 8,000$$
Problem 6
Which two sides of this pentagon appear to be parallel?

Answer: Segment BC and Segment DE (or ED)
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • Parallel lines are lines that never intersect and remain the same distance apart.
  • Looking at the pentagon, the vertical wall on the right (\( \overline{BC} \)) and the vertical wall on the left (\( \overline{DE} \)) are perfectly straight up and down.
  • Because they run strictly vertically side-by-side, they are parallel.

Problem 8
A square with a perimeter of 60 cm has sides that are how many centimeters long?
Answer: 15 cm
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • A square has 4 sides of equal length. The perimeter is the total distance around all 4 sides.
  • Divide the total perimeter by 4:
    $$60 \div 4 = 15 \text{ cm}$$
Problem 9
Segment AB is 19 mm long. Segment BC is 17 mm long. Segment AD is 64 mm long. How long is segment CD?

Answer: 28 mm
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • The entire segment AD is made up of segments AB, BC, and CD added together.
  • Set up the equation: \( AB + BC + CD = AD \)
    $$19 + 17 + CD = 64$$
  • Combine the known lengths: \( 19 + 17 = 36 \)
  • Subtract this sum from the total length to find CD:
    $$64 - 36 = 28 \text{ mm}$$

Problem 16
Simplify: \( 6^2 - \sqrt{36} \)
Answer: 30
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • Evaluate the exponent (6 squared means \( 6 \times 6 \)):
    $$6^2 = 36$$
  • Evaluate the square root (what number times itself is 36?):
    $$\sqrt{36} = 6$$
  • Subtract the two values:
    $$36 - 6 = 30$$

Answer: 24,000

Problem 20
Find the missing number:
  5
  5
  7
 18
  5
+ n
---
 59
Answer: 19
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • First, add all the known numbers together:
    $$5 + 5 + 7 + 18 + 5 = 40$$
  • The problem becomes: \( 40 + n = 59 \)
  • Subtract 40 from 59 to find the missing value \( n \):
    $$59 - 40 = 19$$

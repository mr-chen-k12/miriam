a.

Brainstorm ways to recall the ten memory-group facts. Then complete Power Up F.

Answer: Sample recall strategies (any reasonable ideas count):

  • Elevens: \(11 \times 11 = 121\) — the digits double up; \(11 \times 12 = 132\) is just 11 more than 121.
  • Break 12 apart: \(12 \times n = (10 \times n) + (2 \times n)\). Example: \(12 \times 7 = 70 + 14 = 84\).
  • Dozen facts: \(12 \times 12 = 144\) — "a dozen dozen"; \(12 \times 5 = 60\) — five dozen is 60.
  • Tens: multiplying by 10 just tacks on a zero: \(10 \times 12 = 120\).

Written Practice

1 Formulate

There were two hundred twenty boats on the river. There were four hundred five boats in the harbor. How many more boats were in the harbor? Write and solve an equation.

Answer: 185 more boats

Solution: Let \(b\) = boats more in the harbor. Equation: \(220 + b = 405\), so \(b = 405 - 220 = 185\). Check: \(220 + 185 = 405\) ✓

2 Represent

Five hundred seventy-five thousand, five hundred forty-two people lived in the city. Use digits to write that number of people.

Answer: 575,542

Solution: "Five hundred seventy-five thousand" = 575,000; "five hundred forty-two" = 542. Together: 575,542.

3 Represent

Write 2503 in expanded form. Then use words to write the number.

Answer: Expanded form: \(2000 + 500 + 3\). In words: two thousand, five hundred three.

Solution: 2503 has 2 thousands, 5 hundreds, 0 tens, and 3 ones, so \(2503 = 2000 + 500 + 0 + 3 = 2000 + 500 + 3\).

4 Model

On 1-cm grid paper, draw a rectangle 6 cm long and 4 cm wide.

a. What is the perimeter of the rectangle?

b. What is the area of the rectangle?

Answer: a. 20 cm   b. 24 square cm

Solution: a. Perimeter \(= 2 \times (6 + 4) = 2 \times 10 = 20\) cm.
b. Area \(= \text{length} \times \text{width} = 6 \times 4 = 24\) square centimeters (count the 24 one-centimeter squares inside).

5 Represent

To what mixed number is the arrow pointing?

Answer: \(8\frac{3}{4}\)

Solution: Each unit is divided into 4 equal parts, so each small step is \(\frac{1}{4}\). The arrow is 3 quarter-steps past 8: \(8 + \frac{3}{4} = 8\frac{3}{4}\).

6 Conclude

Which street is parallel to Broad Street?

Answer: Main Street

Solution: Parallel streets run in the same direction and never meet. Broad Street and Main Street are both drawn as horizontal lines going the same way, so Main Street is parallel to Broad Street (the cross street runs north–south, perpendicular to both).

7

What mixed number is shown by the shaded circles?

Answer: \(2\frac{1}{8}\)

Solution: Two whole circles are fully shaded. The third circle is divided into 8 equal parts with 1 part shaded, so it shows \(\frac{1}{8}\). Together: \(2 + \frac{1}{8} = 2\frac{1}{8}\).

8

a. Round 624 to the nearest ten.

b. Round $6.24 to the nearest dollar.

c. Round $6.24 to the nearest 25 cents.

Answer: a. 620   b. $6   c. $6.25

Solution: a. 624 is between 620 and 630; the ones digit 4 is less than 5, so round down to 620.
b. $6.24 is between $6 and $7; the cents (24¢) are less than 50¢, so round down to $6.
c. $6.24 is between $6.00 and $6.25; it is only 1¢ away from $6.25, so round up to $6.25.

9

On a school day, Alberto finishes eating breakfast every morning at the time shown on the clock. He starts eating lunch at 12:30 p.m. How long after Alberto finishes eating breakfast does he start eating lunch?

Answer: 5 hours 5 minutes

Solution: The clock shows 7:25 (hour hand between 7 and 8, minute hand on the 5). From 7:25 to 12:25 is 5 hours; from 12:25 to 12:30 is 5 more minutes. Total: 5 hours 5 minutes.

12 Connect

This square illustrates six squared. What multiplication fact is illustrated by the square?

Answer: \(6 \times 6 = 36\)

Solution: "Six squared" means \(6^2 = 6 \times 6\). The square is a 6-by-6 array: 6 rows of 6, which is 36 small squares in all.

Compare: \(\frac{1}{10}\) of a dollar \(\bigcirc\) \(\frac{1}{2}\) of a dollar

Answer: \(\frac{1}{10} < \frac{1}{2}\)

17

\(\begin{array}{r} \$7.23 \\ -\ \$2.54 \\ \hline \end{array}\)

Answer: $4.69

Solution: Line up the decimal points and subtract with borrowing: \[\begin{array}{r} 7.23 \\ -\ 2.54 \\ \hline 4.69 \end{array}\] \(3 - 4\) needs a borrow; working through gives \(7.23 - 2.54 = 4.69\).

18

\(\begin{array}{r} \$5.42 \\ +\ \$2.69 \\ \hline \end{array}\)

Answer: $8.11

Solution: Line up the decimal points and add: \[\begin{array}{r} 5.42 \\ +\ 2.69 \\ \hline 8.11 \end{array}\] \(2 + 9 = 11\) (write 1, carry 1); \(4 + 6 + 1 = 11\) (write 1, carry 1); \(5 + 2 + 1 = 8\).

22

If the radius of a circle is 100 cm, then the diameter of the circle is how many meters?

Answer: 2 meters

Solution: The diameter is twice the radius: \(2 \times 100 = 200\) cm. Since 100 cm = 1 m, \(200 \text{ cm} = 2\) meters.

23

\(28 + 36 + 78 + \sqrt{49}\)

Answer: 149

Solution: \(\sqrt{49} = 7\). Then \(28 + 36 + 78 + 7\): \(28 + 36 = 64\); \(64 + 78 = 142\); \(142 + 7 = 149\).

24

\(\sqrt{144} - \sqrt{121}\)

Answer: 1

Solution: \(\sqrt{144} = 12\) because \(12 \times 12 = 144\), and \(\sqrt{121} = 11\) because \(11 \times 11 = 121\). So \(12 - 11 = 1\).

25 Multiple Choice

Which of the following is not \(\frac{1}{10}\) of a dollar?

  1. dime
  2. 0.10¢
  3. $0.10
  4. 10¢

Answer: B — 0.10¢

Solution: \(\frac{1}{10}\) of a dollar is 10 cents. A dime = 10¢ ✓, $0.10 = 10¢ ✓, 10¢ ✓. But 0.10¢ means one-tenth of one cent — far less than 10 cents — so B is not \(\frac{1}{10}\) of a dollar.

26 Multiple Choice

Which digit in 457,326,180 is in the hundred-thousands place?

  1. 1
  2. 6
  3. 4
  4. 3

Answer: D — 3

Solution: Count places from the right in 457,326,180: ones (0), tens (8), hundreds (1), thousands (6), ten-thousands (2), hundred-thousands (3), millions (7), ten-millions (5), hundred-millions (4). The hundred-thousands digit is 3.

27 Conclude

Name and describe each angle below.

Answer: a. Right angle   b. Obtuse angle   c. Acute angle

Solution: a. The square corner mark means the angle is exactly 90° — a right angle.
b. The angle opens wider than 90° — an obtuse angle (greater than 90° but less than 180°).
c. The angle opens narrower than 90° — an acute angle (less than 90°).

28

Consider the sequence 12, 24, 36, 48, …

a. Generalize Write a rule that describes how to find the next term of the sequence.

b. Predict What is the eleventh term of the sequence?

Answer: a. Add 12 to get the next term (count by 12s).   b. 132

Solution: a. Each term is 12 more than the one before: \(24 - 12 = 12\), \(36 - 24 = 12\), \(48 - 36 = 12\). Rule: add 12.
b. The terms are multiples of 12: term 1 = \(12 \times 1\), term 2 = \(12 \times 2\), …, so term 11 = \(12 \times 11 = 132\).

29 Estimate

Mrs. Rojas would like to purchase a CD that costs $14.99 and a DVD that costs $18.95 for the school library. What is a reasonable estimate of the amount of money she will spend? Explain why your estimate is reasonable.

Answer: About $34

Solution: Round each price to the nearest dollar: $14.99 rounds to $15 and $18.95 rounds to $19. Then \(\$15 + \$19 = \$34\). This is reasonable because each rounded price is within a few cents of the actual price, and the exact total (\$33.94) is very close to $34.

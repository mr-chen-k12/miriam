1.(31)
FORMULATE Write and solve equations for problems 1 and 2.
There were four hundred seventy-two birds in the first flock. There were one hundred forty-seven birds in the second flock. How many fewer birds were in the second flock?
\(472 - 147 = \)
Answer
\(325\text{ fewer birds}\)
Solution
Equation: \(147 + n = 472\) or \(472 - 147 = n\). Subtracting: \(472 - 147 = 325\). The second flock had 325 fewer birds.

3.(47)
CONNECT Write four multiplication/division facts using the numbers 3, 5, and 15.
Answer
\(3 \times 5 = 15, \quad 5 \times 3 = 15, \quad 15 \div 3 = 5, \quad 15 \div 5 = 3\)
Solution
The two smaller numbers are the factors and the largest is the product: \(3 \times 5 = 15\) and \(5 \times 3 = 15\). Turning them around gives the division facts: \(15 \div 3 = 5\) and \(15 \div 5 = 3\).
4.(10)
Use the digits 1, 3, 6, and 8 to write an odd number between 8000 and 8350. Each digit may be used only once.
Answer
\(8163\)
Solution
An odd number must end in 1 or 3, and to stay between 8000 and 8350 it must start with 8. Checking the possibilities: 8136 is even, 8316 is even, and 8361 is over 8350 — leaving 8163, which is odd and between 8000 and 8350. It is the only number that works.

24.(46)
Simplify: \(6\sqrt{54}\)
Answer
\(\text{about }44\)
Solution
54 is not a perfect square: \(7^2 = 49\) and \(8^2 = 64\), so \(\sqrt{54}\) is between 7 and 8, about 7.35. Then \(6 \times 7.35 \approx 44.1\), so \(6\sqrt{54}\) is about 44. (Note: because 54 is not a perfect square, this answer is an approximation, not an exact whole number.)

26.(Inv. 3)
MULTIPLE CHOICE A rectangle has an area of 12 sq. in. Which of these could not be the length and width of the rectangle?
A 4 in. by 3 in.   B 6 in. by 2 in.
C 12 in. by 1 in.   D 4 in. by 2 in.
Answer
\(\text{D}\)
Solution
Check each area: A: \(4 \times 3 = 12\) ✓; B: \(6 \times 2 = 12\) ✓; C: \(12 \times 1 = 12\) ✓; D: \(4 \times 2 = 8\), not 12 ✗. So D could not be the length and width.
27.(45)
JUSTIFY Which property of multiplication is shown here?
\(5 \times (2 \times 7) = (5 \times 2) \times 7\)
Answer
\(\text{Associative Property of Multiplication}\)
Solution
Only the grouping changed — the factors 5, 2, and 7 stayed in the same order — and the product stayed the same. That is the Associative Property of Multiplication.
28.(47)
Use digits and three different division symbols to show “twenty-four divided by three.”
Answer
\(24 \div 3, \quad \frac{24}{3}, \quad 3 \enclose{longdiv}{24}\)
Solution
The three ways to write 24 divided by 3: the division sign (\(24 \div 3\)), the fraction bar (\(\frac{24}{3}\)), and the long-division bracket (\(3 \enclose{longdiv}{24}\), with 3 outside and 24 inside). All three equal 8.
29.(48)
ESTIMATE D'Ron mailed nine invitations and placed a 39¢ stamp on each invitation. Estimate the total postage cost for the 9 invitations. Explain how you estimated the total.
Answer
\(\text{About }\$3.60\)
Solution
Round each 39¢ stamp up to 40¢, an easy number to multiply: \(9 \times 40\text{¢} = 360\text{¢} = \$3.60\). So the postage cost about $3.60. (The exact cost is \(9 \times 39\text{¢} = 351\text{¢} = \$3.51\), close to the estimate.)
